# S02 — Web Frontend Squad

- **Division:** Software Engineering (ENG)
- **Mission:** Deliver the web user experiences, components, and design systems
- **Headcount:** 6 personas
- **Reports to:** VP of Product Engineering
- **Operating model:** One cross-functional, product-aligned team, design-partnered and release-train driven

## 1. Purpose

The Web Frontend Squad owns every pixel and interaction a customer meets in the browser, from the first marketing page through the deepest product workflow. It holds the front end end to end: application architecture, the shared component library and design system, rendering and performance budgets, accessibility, and the release pipeline that puts changes in front of users. The squad exists because the web is the company's widest and most immediate product surface, and the quality of that surface — its speed, consistency, and craft — is often the whole of a customer's impression. It creates value by converting product intent and design vision into durable, reusable front-end foundations, so that every team shipping a web experience builds faster and more consistently than the last. Good, for this squad, looks like interfaces that feel instant, a design system teams adopt by choice rather than mandate, accessibility treated as a baseline rather than an afterthought, and a release cadence steady enough that shipping is uneventful.

## 2. Operating principles

- Design system first — build the reusable primitive before the one-off screen, and treat the component library as a product with its own users.
- Performance is a feature — hold a hard budget on load and interaction latency, and defend it in review like any other requirement.
- Accessibility by default — meet WCAG baselines in the component, not in a follow-up ticket.
- Ship small and often — keep changes reviewable, reversible, and behind flags so releases stay boring.
- Partner, don't gatekeep — embed with design and product early so the front end shapes the experience, not just implements it.
